\subsection*{4.1 The Pillars of Architecture}
As the Game Architect, your word is final - but your primary job is to protect the table's collective fun, not win tactical arguments. Commit these laws to memory:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rule 1: Guard Table Enjoyment.} Keep every player engaged, especially quieter people.
  \item \textbf{Rule 2: Refer Directly to Rule 1.}
  \item \textbf{Rule 3: Momentum Over Correctness.} If a rules question slows the story, make a quick ruling and keep moving. Look up the official answer later.
\end{itemize}

\DndrCallout{The Narrative Trick}{Use isolated, vivid sensory details instead of long lore. Choose one anchor: smell (ozone, wet rot), sound (distant rhythmic chanting), or texture (grit between teeth). If stuck, ask a player what happens next, then build on the answer.}

\DndrCallout{Failure Example}{The jungle quest fails one hero at a time, but the party wakes in a dungeon that smells like rotting flesh: the necromancer's fortress. Failure changed the path instead of ending the story.}

\subsection*{4.2 Ending a Quest}
Reveal all cards and read them aloud. Choose the outcome that creates the most entertaining, dramatic, surprising, or satisfying continuation. There is no single correct answer.

\textbf{Success:} The party persuades the NPC, defeats the monster, or solves the puzzle. Narrate how success leads to the next Quest.

\textbf{Failure:} The negotiation fails, trap triggers, or monsters defeat the party. Narrate the setback and redirect the party toward the next Quest.

\textbf{Rewards:} Deal 1 Item Card to each player, then draw additional Item Cards equal to twice the Challenge Rating and distribute them among the party.

\subsection*{4.3 Running Diplomacy Quests}
\begin{enumerate}
  \DndrStep{Present the NPC:}{Draw an NPC Card and introduce the character the party must persuade.}
  \DndrStep{Set the Challenge:}{Draw 1-3 Challenge Cards. Each player explains how their Challenge complicates the Quest.}
  \DndrStep{Deal:}{Give every player except the Architect 3 Diplomacy Cards.}
  \DndrStep{Submit:}{Each player submits 1 card. Add random cards equal to the Challenge Rating and shuffle.}
  \DndrStep{Activate Items:}{Players may activate Items normally. Table talk is encouraged: discuss, negotiate, argue, joke, and advocate.}
  \DndrStep{The Architect's Verdict:}{Read all cards and choose the outcome that best continues the story.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{4.5 Running Combat Quests}
Combat Quests are cooperative battles where the party races to defeat all monsters before the monsters defeat the party.

\begin{enumerate}
  \DndrStep{Set Up the Encounter:}{Place the required Monster Cards and set their health from the stat blocks.}
  \DndrStep{Monsters Act:}{Monsters always act first. Roll the attack die, compare it to the target's Defense, and deal listed damage on a hit.}
  \DndrStep{Players Act:}{Starting left of the Architect, each player takes one turn: Basic Attack for half a Heart, Activate an Item, or Charge to double the next attack.}
  \DndrStep{Instant Items:}{Green Items may be played outside a turn when their trigger occurs and do not consume the turn's action.}
  \DndrStep{Repeat:}{Alternate monster and player turns until one side is defeated.}
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Victory:} Resolve the Quest normally and gain rewards.\\
\textbf{Defeat:} Resolve the Quest as a failure. Defeated heroes recover to half health before the next Quest.
