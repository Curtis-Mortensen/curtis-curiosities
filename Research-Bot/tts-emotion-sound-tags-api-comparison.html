1. Introduction & aims

Modern neural text-to-speech (TTS) no longer only “reads the words.” A growing set of APIs let you write stage directions into the script — emotion words, delivery cues, and non-verbal sounds such as laughs, sighs, gasps, or breaths — so a single generation pass can sound acted rather than flat.[1][2]

This report starts from a practical baseline: personal projects currently using Gemini TTS, which supports scene direction, voice/audio tags, noises, and about 30 prebuilt voices, but costs more than desired.[3][4] Recent alternatives of interest include xAI Grok TTS / flagship voices, Fish Audio S2.1, and Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS, with ElevenLabs as the familiar premium foil.[5][6][7]

The document aims to:

  1. Define a strict filter: models that support emotion/delivery tags and sound tags like laughs.
  2. Produce a fairly comprehensive catalog of releases from roughly the last year (≈ Jul 2025–Jul 2026) that meet that filter — hosted and open-source/open-weight.
  3. Normalize published pricing for billable APIs so Gemini, Grok, Fish, Qwen, ElevenLabs, Inworld, MiniMax, Cartesia, and peers can be compared on cost.
  4. Check whether the newest releases actually ship tag support — or whether new models are “dumb voices” again.
  5. Cover OpenAI’s recent voice/TTS updates against the laugh-tag filter.
  6. Pull Artificial Analysis Speech Arena Elo for the top five models and weave Elo into comparisons.
  7. Give a practical recommendation for cost-sensitive personal projects that refuse to give up laughs and emotion cues.
Quick verdict (after a broader market sweep): The first pass understated the market. Counting distinct product lines that meet the emotion + laugh/sound-tag filter across ~12 months yields roughly 9–11 hosted/closed APIs and roughly 6–8 open-source / open-weight systems (plus Step-Audio-EditX as a tag-based post-editor). That is on the order of ~15–18 named systems, not seven.[36][37][38][39][40] Newest closed releases (Grok, Fish S2.1, Qwen-Audio-3.0, Inworld TTS-2, Eleven v3, Gemini 3.1, StepAudio 2.5) largely do ship tags. Artificial Analysis top 5 Elo (2026-07-30): Simba 1,229 · Qwen Plus 1,227 · Gemini 3.1 1,213 · Luna 1,203 · Sonic 3.5 1,202 — with OpenAI’s steerable gpt-4o-mini-tts important but not native laugh-tag. On hosted list price, Fish (~$15/M bytes) and Grok ($15/M chars) still undercut Gemini 3.1 and crush ElevenLabs v3 ($100/M); self-hosting Orpheus / Maya1 / CosyVoice 3 / Dia can push marginal cost toward GPU electricity instead of API fees.

3. Limitations & strength of evidence

Theoretical / market limitations

TTS quality is partly subjective: “sounds human” and “the laugh landed” are listener judgments, not single lab metrics. Vendor demos cherry-pick good takes; tag obedience varies by voice, language, and how crowded the script is with cues.[16] Pricing pages change without academic archival discipline — a July 2026 snapshot can be wrong by September.

Methodology limitations

  • This report relies on vendor documentation, pricing pages, launch blogs, HF cards, papers, and secondary roundups. It does not run a blind listening study or burn real API spend across every vendor.
  • The first draft under-counted open-source and some hosted peers; this revision widens the sweep but the market still moves weekly — treat the catalog as a July 2026 snapshot, not a permanent census.
  • Billing units differ (characters vs UTF-8 bytes vs audio tokens). Normalized “$ per 1M English characters” and “$ per audio minute” are estimates using published conversion assumptions (e.g., Google’s ~25 audio tokens/second).[13]
  • Some tag lists are incomplete in third-party mirrors (HTML stripping of angle-bracket wrapping tags). Prefer primary docs when integrating.
  • Open-source / self-hosted models are cataloged for capability; cost ranks GPU/ops separately from hosted $/character ladders.

Field maturity

Expressive neural TTS with inline performance tags is a young product category (roughly 2024–2026 for the current “audio tag” wave). Independent comparative science is thinner than for ASR word-error-rate. Treat Elo leaderboards and marketing “#1” claims as useful signals, not final truth.[17]

4. History of the fields that try to answer this

Speech synthesis has cycled through control paradigms for decades:

Timeline (simplified) 1990s–2000s Concatenative / formant TTS Control = pitch/duration params, limited expressivity ~2004–2015 SSML (W3C) becomes the enterprise "tag language" <break>, <prosody>, <emphasis> — emotion often vendor-specific ~2016–2020 Neural WaveNet / Tacotron era Better naturalness; control often via style tokens / embeddings (not friendly inline script tags for hobbyists) ~2021–2024 Voice cloning APIs (ElevenLabs et al.) Emotion often "hope the model infers it" or separate emotion enums ~2024–2026 "Audio tags" / "speech tags" / "sound tags" product wave Bracket cues like [laughs] [whisper] [angry] written into the script Gemini 3.1, Eleven v3, Fish S2/S2.1, Grok TTS, Qwen-Audio-3.0, MiniMax 2.8

SSML taught the industry that markup belongs next to the words.[18] The 2025–2026 wave replaces much of that XML with natural-language brackets that models learned from training data — sometimes as free-form text (Fish S2.1) rather than a closed enum.[6] That is why “does the new model support tags?” became a buyer question overnight: without tags, expressive pipelines regress to regenerate-and-pray.

5. Inclusion filter: what “tags” means here

To match the user’s requirement — emotion tags and sound tags like laughs — a model must document both of the following in primary or close-secondary docs:

  1. Emotion / delivery tags — inline or SSML markers that steer mood or delivery (e.g., [angry], [excited], <emotion value="sad"/>, wrapping <whisper>…</whisper>).
  2. Non-verbal sound tags — cues that produce or strongly trigger vocal sounds such as laughs, chuckles, sighs, gasps, breaths, coughs (e.g., [laughs], [laugh], (laughs), [laughter]).
Excluded by design: APIs that only offer a separate “emotion” API parameter with no laugh/sound cues; APIs that only take a free-text “acting description” field without documented inline laugh tags (notably Hume Octave’s description-based acting instructions); plain TTS with no documented tag vocabulary (classic OpenAI TTS tiers without audio tags).[19]

8. OpenAI voice updates — do they meet the tag filter?

Short answer: OpenAI did ship a meaningful TTS upgrade — gpt-4o-mini-tts with natural-language instructions for accent, emotional range, tone, whispering, etc. — plus later snapshots (community discussion of a 2025-12-15 default) and Realtime / custom-voice work.[52][53] But it does not document native inline sound tags like [laughs]. Emotion is steered via a separate instructions string (or SDK shims that map brackets → instructions), not Gemini/Fish-style script tags. So OpenAI stays in the near-miss / adjacent bucket for this report’s dual filter — important market context, not a drop-in tag replacement.

What shipped

  • gpt-4o-mini-tts — steerable TTS; docs highlight control of accent, emotional range, intonation, impressions, speed, tone, whispering via instructions.[52]
  • Built-in voice set expanded (docs list ~13 including marin/cedar as quality picks); older tts-1 / tts-1-hd remain.
  • Custom voices (eligible customers) via consent + sample upload for Speech / Realtime / Chat audio.
  • Realtime path: GPT-Realtime-2 appears on Artificial Analysis at Elo 1,064 (AA list price ~$191.6/1M chars equivalent) — speech-to-speech / realtime agent stack, not the tagged narration API.[51]
  • Community reports that snapshot gpt-4o-mini-tts-2025-12-15 changed instruction-following vs …-2025-03-20 (quality vs obedience trade-offs).[54]

Why it fails the laugh-tag bar

Official examples use instructions like “Speak in a cheerful and positive tone” — whole-utterance direction. There is no documented vocabulary of inline non-verbals ([laugh], [sigh]) that insert audio events mid-script the way Gemini, Fish, Eleven v3, Grok, Qwen, Inworld, or Dia do. Third-party SDKs sometimes strip [cheerfully] tags and rewrite them into instructions, which is an app-layer workaround, not native sound tags.[55]

Elo vs your tagged shortlist

On the AA board: TTS-1 HD Elo 1,102, TTS-1 Elo 1,083 — well below Gemini 3.1 (1,213) and Qwen Plus (1,227). OpenAI’s advantage is ecosystem convenience and instruction steerability, not arena-top quality or laugh tags.[51]

11. Hosted API deep-dives

9.1 Google Gemini TTS baseline 30 voices

Gemini TTS is the user’s current stack: prebuilt voice library of 30 voices, multi-speaker dialogue, natural-language Audio Profile / Scene / Director’s Notes, and — especially on Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS — a large vocabulary of inline audio tags (Google Cloud materials cite 200+).[1][3][4]

Common tags documented in the speech-generation guide include emotion and delivery markers plus non-verbals:

[laughs][giggles][sighs][gasp][cough] [whispers][excited][sarcastic][shouting]

Billing: not per character. Paid Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS is $1 / 1M text input tokens and $20 / 1M audio output tokens, with audio metered at 25 tokens per second (≈ $0.03 per second-minute of audio before text input).[13] Gemini 2.5 Flash TTS is half that on both sides ($0.50 / $10).[13] Batch API discounts apply on some surfaces.[28]

Best for: scene-level direction + multi-speaker scenes when you already live in Google’s ecosystem. Pain point: 3.1’s expressivity tier is the one that matches “tags + noises,” and it costs roughly 2× 2.5 Flash on audio tokens.

9.2 xAI Grok TTS / flagship voices new

xAI’s Text-to-Speech API converts text to audio with built-in and custom voices, streaming options, and explicit speech tags. Official docs show inline cues such as [pause], [laugh], and wrapping delivery tags such as <whisper>…</whisper>.[8] Telnyx’s mirror of the tag set lists laughs/chuckles/giggles, sighs/breaths, and wrapping volume/pitch/pace controls.[29]

On 2026-07-06, xAI announced 21 new flagship multilingual voices joining the original five (Ara, Eve, Leo, Rex, Sal), with speech tags called out for delivery control — about 26 built-in voices in the marketing lineup, plus cloning from ~1 minute of audio.[5]

Billing: $15.00 per 1 million characters on the Voice pricing table.[12][30] That is roughly half Gemini 3.1’s effective character-equivalent cost and about 1/6.7 of ElevenLabs v3 list price.

Caveat: Tag vocabulary looks smaller/more fixed than Gemini’s 200+ or Fish’s open-domain brackets. Scene-level “director worldbuilding” is weaker than Gemini’s Scene + Audio Profile stack. Still clearly meets emotion + laugh requirements.

11.3 Fish Audio S2.1 Pro new cost leader

Fish positions S2.1-Pro as the production model: ~100 ms time-to-first-audio claims, 83 languages, multi-speaker, and open-domain bracket tags learned as natural language — not a closed enum — e.g. [whispers sweetly], [laughing nervously].[6][31] Emotion docs enumerate laughs, chuckles, sobs, sighs, audience laughter, and many emotion words.[9]

Billing: $15 / million UTF-8 bytes for s2.1-pro (and prior S2/S1 paid strings); s2.1-pro-free is $0 under fair-use, without TTFA/DPA guarantees, with free-window messaging evolving through mid/late 2026.[11][15]

Best for: personal projects that want Gemini-like bracket directionality at Fish/Grok price points — especially while the free model string remains usable. Watch: fair-use / commercial ARR clauses on the free tier; UTF-8 byte billing bites harder on non-Latin scripts.

11.4 Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS new yes, tags

Yes — Qwen released a production audio TTS line. Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS launched around 2026-07-20 as hosted Flash (~300 ms first-packet positioning) and Plus (quality) models on Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, without public weight downloads for this hosted line.[7][32]

Controllability is dual-track: free-style natural-language style instructions plus 86 fine-grained inline tags for localized emotion/style and non-verbal events (laughter, breathing, coughing, sighing, etc.).[10] Examples in launch materials include [angry], [giggles], [laughing], [gasp].[7]

Billing: widely reported at about $27.59–$27.60 per million characters via Model Studio — between Gemini 2.5 Flash’s effective rate and Gemini 3.1’s, and far below ElevenLabs/MiniMax HD.[17][25] Plus debuted at or near the top of Artificial Analysis Speech Arena coverage (~1,236 Elo in press).[17]

Trade-offs: slower throughput in some reports (~16 chars/sec for Plus), 16 languages (strong Chinese dialect coverage), hosted-only, and some streaming-mode caveats for tag support noted in secondary guides.[32]

11.5 ElevenLabs Eleven v3 premium

Eleven v3 is the tag-capable ElevenLabs tier: bracket audio tags for emotions ([curious], [crying]), delivery ([whispers], [shouts]), and human reactions ([laughs], [clears throat], [sighs]), plus dialogue APIs.[2][33]

Billing: Multilingual v2/v3 API list rate $0.10 per 1,000 characters (= $100 / 1M), with Flash/Turbo at $0.05 / 1K for the low-latency non-v3 path.[14] This is the expensive end of the tag club — and the reference that makes Gemini look “cheap” by comparison.

11.6 MiniMax Speech 2.8

Speech 2.8 adds Sound Tags injected in parentheses — (laughs), (chuckle), (sighs), (gasps), breathing and articulations — alongside emotion presets.[21] Official pay-as-you-go lists speech-2.8-turbo at $60 / 1M characters and speech-2.8-hd at $100 / 1M.[22]

Functionally in the club; commercially closer to ElevenLabs than to Fish/Grok/Gemini.

11.7 Cartesia Sonic

Sonic supports emotion via generation_config.emotion or SSML <emotion value="…"/>, and nonverbal laughter via inserting [laughter] in the transcript.[16][23] Emotion control is documented as beta/experimental for mid-utterance shifts. Pricing is credit-based (~1 credit per character); secondary guides place PAYG near ~$50 / 1M characters.[26][27]

Qualifies under the filter, but the laugh vocabulary is thinner than Gemini/Fish/Eleven/Qwen.

11.8 Inworld Realtime TTS-2 new

Inworld’s Realtime TTS-2 (research preview, 2026-05-05) treats delivery like an LLM prompt: natural-language steering instructions in square brackets, plus inline non-verbals [laugh], [sigh], [breathe], [cough], [yawn], [clear throat].[36][43] Prior TTS 1.5 models had a smaller experimental emotion markup set; full steering is TTS-2-only.

Billing: plan-tiered per million characters (published bands roughly $12.50–$35 / 1M depending on commitment).[44] Competes with Fish/Grok on the cheap-expressive band for agent workloads.

11.9 MyVocal v3

MyVocal’s v3 path documents prompt control via [tag] syntax covering emotions (happy, excited, sad, angry…) and non-speech sounds (laughter, throat clearing, yawning, sighing, gasping, crying…).[45] Include it in a comprehensive market list; verify current retail pricing in their console before budgeting.

12. Open-source / open-weight deep-dives

These are the self-host options that meet (or nearly meet) the dual tag bar. Marginal cost is GPU/ops rather than $/character — which is why personal projects often end here once scripts get long.

12.1 Orpheus TTS (Canopy Labs)

Released March 2025 (multilingual research family April 2025). Llama-3B speech-LLM with inline emotive tags: laugh, chuckle, sigh, cough, sniffle, groan, yawn, gasp.[38] Emotion is primarily expressed through those paralinguistic tags plus voice choice rather than a huge [angry]/[sarcastic] lexicon like Gemini 3.1 — but the product explicitly markets “guided emotion and intonation,” and the laugh/sigh path is first-class.

12.2 Maya1 (Maya Research)

Production-ready positioning around November 2025. 3B Llama-style + SNAC codec; natural-language voice design; 20+ inline emotion tags including laugh, cry, whisper, sigh, gasp, rage.[37] Among open models, this is one of the closest philosophical matches to Gemini/Eleven-style bracket directionality.

12.3 CosyVoice 2 / CosyVoice 3 (FunAudioLLM)

CosyVoice 2 already demonstrated [laughter] fine-grained control and instruct prompts; CosyVoice 3 scales instruction types (100+) and keeps vocal-burst markers such as [laughter] / [breath] plus emphasis wraps.[39][46] Open-sourced CosyVoice 3 weights landed later in 2025. Strong choice if you want open weights with Chinese dialect depth plus laugh tags.

12.4 Dia 1.6B (Nari Labs)

Dialogue-native open TTS with speaker tags [S1]/[S2] and a long parenthetical nonverbal list: (laughs), (sighs), (coughs), (gasps), etc.[40] Emotion/tone steering is primarily via audio conditioning / cloning, not a large text emotion lexicon — so it is a strong sound-tag model and a partial emotion-tag model. Still essential on any “last year” list for podcasts/sketches that need real laughs.

12.5 Step-Audio-EditX

Not a TTS by itself: a 3B audio editing LLM (StepFun) that applies emotion, style, and paralinguistic tags — including laughter — as a second pass over audio from many engines.[41] Important because it brings tag-like control to engines that lack native laughs (F5-TTS, etc.). Released/updated late 2025 into 2026.

12.6 Chatterbox (Resemble AI) — provisional

Open-sourced May 2025 with emotion exaggeration controls; later multilingual. Community tooling documents experimental native sound/emotion tokens, but also warns they can be incomplete versus Step-Audio-EditX post-edits.[47] Keep on the radar; do not plan a production laugh pipeline on undocumented tokens alone.

15. Conclusion

Did the first pass miss market options? Yes. A Gemini/Grok/Fish/Qwen/Eleven-centric shortlist understated the field. A last-year sweep finds roughly 9–11 hosted/closed and 6–8 open systems that clear (or nearly clear) emotion + laugh/sound tags — about 15–18 named systems total (plus StepAudio 2.5’s parenthetical direction).

OpenAI? Yes, they updated voice TTS (gpt-4o-mini-tts + snapshots + Realtime/custom voices). Steerability is via instructions, not native [laughs] tags — so it remains a near-miss for this filter. Arena Elo sits well below the tagged leaders (TTS-1 HD 1,102 vs Gemini 1,213 / Qwen 1,227).

Arena top 5 Elo (2026-07-30): Simba 3.2 1,229 · Qwen-Audio-3.0-Plus 1,227 · Gemini 3.1 Flash 1,213 · Luna 1,203 · Sonic 3.5 1,202. Three of five are tag-capable.

Hosted additions worth knowing: Inworld TTS-2 and MyVocal v3 join Fish, Grok, Gemini, Eleven v3, Qwen-Audio-3.0, MiniMax 2.8, and Cartesia. Doubao Seed-TTS 2.0 is a verify-before-relying member.

Open-source additions: Orpheus, Maya1, CosyVoice 2/3, Dia, Step-Audio-EditX (edit layer), provisional Chatterbox, and Fish S1-mini weights. These are the real “cheaper than I’d like Gemini to be” path if you can run a GPU.

Tags on new releases: Still good news — the expressive launches of the last year mostly ship tags. Disappointing would be launching a “premium voice” with no laughs; that is not what Grok / Fish 2.1 / Qwen / Inworld / Maya1 did.

Cost: Gemini remains cheaper than ElevenLabs v3. Fish/Grok (~$15) and often Inworld undercut Gemini 3.1. Self-host Maya1/Orpheus/CosyVoice/Dia can beat all of them on marginal cost for personal volume.

Further research questions

  • Blind A/B tag obedience: Maya1 vs Gemini 3.1 vs Fish S2.1 on the same laugh+emotion script.
  • Dia nonverbal fidelity vs Eleven v3 on podcast-style (laughs).
  • True all-in cost of self-hosting Orpheus/Maya1 including idle GPU vs Fish free/paid.
  • Doubao COT tag parser reliability for laughs on cloned vs stock voices.
  • Whether Chatterbox native tokens improve enough to leave “provisional.”
